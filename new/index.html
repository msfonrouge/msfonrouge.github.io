I build software that takes repetitive work off people's hands.

Invoices nobody had time to load. Stock that left one place and never arrived at the next. Quotes typed by hand, one at a time. Messages from customers that waited days for an answer.

I sit with the people who do the work, find where the hours really go, and fix that first. Below are some things I built, with the results they had.

Selected work

Most of these come from the food and events business: kitchens, stores, and a team that sells events.

Purchasing

Every invoice on the books

50% → 100%of a month's supplier invoices in the accounting system, matching the tax authority's total exactly.

Problem

The tax authority listed every invoice that suppliers had issued. The accounting system had only half of them, because invoices got in only when someone took a photo of the paper copy.

What I built

  • A tool that reads the official list of invoices and compares it with the accounting system.
  • It loads each missing invoice, with the right taxes, for a person to review.
  • It finds invoices that were loaded twice, and fixes small rounding differences.
  • Every run can be undone with one command.

Result

For the first month, the books went from 98 of 195 invoices to 195 of 195. The next two months, 174 more invoices came in the same way.

Costing

Costs you can trust

81ingredient costs repaired, after one product was costed at 16 times what the supplier charged.

Problem

Suppliers bill by the bag or by the box. The system read those lines as if they were per kilo. Potatoes came out at 16 times their real price, and that error was in 65 recipes. Nobody could trust the cost of a dish.

What I built

  • A check that compares each recipe cost with what was actually paid that week.
  • A fix that converts bags and boxes to real units, without changing any invoice total or tax.
  • A test run first, and a way to undo every change.

Result

366 invoice lines on 137 invoices corrected, and 81 ingredient costs repaired. Recipe costs became numbers people could use to set prices.

Stock

Stock that arrives

48 / 48overdue transfers between the warehouse and the stores closed, with a check before and after.

Problem

Goods left the central warehouse, but no store confirmed that they arrived. Thousands of units were stuck "in transit", and the stores showed negative stock.

What I built

  • A tool that receives each transfer with the quantities signed when it left the warehouse.
  • A rule in the system that stops a store from receiving more than was sent.
  • Practice runs on a copy of the data, then the real change at night, with the stores closed.

Result

All 48 overdue transfers closed in one night. The new rule keeps the problem from coming back.

Sales

Proposals in one click

~35 a weekevent proposals that no longer had to be sent by hand.

Problem

A proposal had a button that made the PDF files, and then stopped. Someone still had to find the client in WhatsApp, paste the text and attach every file. The phone number on file was wrong for more than a third of the contacts.

What I built

  • One button that sends the text and all the PDFs to the client on WhatsApp, and notes it on the proposal.
  • It takes the phone number from the real conversation with the client, not from the old contact card.
  • Five safety stops: no phone, no files, already sent, number not on WhatsApp, or a message that failed. A client never gets half a proposal.

Result

At the time, the team sent about 35 proposals a week. Each one became one click instead of a series of manual steps. Of 795 proposals the system wrote over three months, people needed to edit only 15.

Customer inquiries

An answer while the client is still interested

Minutesfrom a new event inquiry to a full proposal on the client's WhatsApp.

Problem

More than a hundred event inquiries came in each month, from the website and from Facebook. Each one waited for someone to read it, make a proposal and send it.

What I built

  • A system that reads each new inquiry and prepares the right proposal.
  • AI reads only the client's free text, to see if it changes anything. Plain rules decide the proposal, so it never invents a price.
  • Anything unclear goes to a person. Replies from clients raise an alert. It stops at the first error.

Result

In a test on 65 real inquiries, 22 could be answered automatically, 11 went to a person, and the rest were not real requests. It has been live since November 2026.

Operations

Where the hours go

128client requests that waited more than a day for an answer, found in three months of records.

Problem

A sales team felt overloaded, and everyone assumed the cause was writing quotes.

What I built

  • A study of 61 working days, put together from messages, documents, the sales system, email and calendars.
  • A simple page that shows, day by day, where the time went.

Result

Quotes took about 6 minutes each. They were not the problem. More than half of the working day left no trace in any system, and 128 real requests waited more than a day. That changed what to fix next.

Product

Tallyfork

Liverecipe costing for restaurants and caterers, built and run by me.

Problem

Kitchens set prices without knowing what a dish really costs. The cost changes every time a supplier changes a price.

What I built

  • Ingredients with suppliers, pack sizes, yield and price history.
  • Recipes inside recipes, scaling, cost per portion and food cost percentage.
  • Menus with a price per guest and a suggested price.
  • Import from a spreadsheet, and a clear reason when a recipe has no cost yet.

Result

A working product, now open by invitation.
